Often overshadowed by his painter and sculptor collaborators, multimedia artist and graphic designer Fortunato Depero was the most prolific maker associated with the Italian Futurist movement.

From the beginning of the First World War to the early 1930s, when Depero withdrew from the movement on the grounds of its increasingly Fascist inclinations, the designer produced an extraordinary variety and quantity of work, from costume and set designs for Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes to advertising campaigns for drinks company, Campari.

Each waistcoat has a unique graphic formula, and was designed by Depero for one individual in particular within the Futurist circle. The image above, taken in 1925, shows Depero (left) wearing a waistcoat made for himself, and founder Italian Futurism, Filippo Tommaso Marinetti (centre), sporting the piece designed for him.
