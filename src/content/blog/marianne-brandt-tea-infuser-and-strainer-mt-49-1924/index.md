Upon completion of the preliminary course in 1924, Weimar Bauhaus student Marianne Brandt was encouraged to join the Metall-Werkstatt (Metal Workshop) by its then leader, Hungarian Constructivist artist, Laszló Moholy-Nagy, who recognised her talent for metalworking.

The Tea Infuser MT 49 was her first major project: paying homage to the simple forms of the circle and square, the design calls to mind contemporaneous teachings advanced by preliminary course master, Johannes Itten, on the metaphysical status of a form adapted from a basic geometrical figure into an object with use value.

Despite initial hostility from her predominately male cohort, who felt that "the metal workshop was no place for a woman", Brandt became one of the single most important members of the workshop, appointed its leader from 1928, and playing an instrumental role in aligning its products with the demands of industrial production.
