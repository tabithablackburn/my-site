Entering production as ‘mobili 4970/84’ after their debut showing at the 1967 Milan Furniture Fair, the Componibili quickly became a Kartell bestseller, and, with their involvement in such decorative campaigns as promoted by Bloomingdale's in New York in 1973, helped to consolidate the Italian company's international success by the early 1970s.

Responding to a 1960s trend in informal living arrangements that pointed to the rising influence of young consumers, the original Componibili, the Italian word for modules, were, in their smallest iteration, available for purchase as individual units that could be stacked and arranged variously as storage towers, bedside tables, bathroom drawers, and other flexible furnishings.

The design's material economy, made possible by its realisation in lightweight, durable, injection-moulded ABS plastic, is characteristic of Kartell's products by the late 1960s; ever since its founding in 1949 by Castelli Ferrieri's chemical engineer husband, Guilio Castelli, the company had set about testing the technological potential of new synthetic materials in the manufacture of affordable everyday objects, beginning with a plastic ski rack for a car rooftop.

Kartell continues to manufacture the Componibili today, though only as fixed, non-stackable structures of two or three tiers each. Examples of earlier versions of the storage unit form part of the permanent collections at the Pompidou Centre in Paris and the Museum of Modern Art in New York, where the Componibili were also shown as early as 1972, on occasion of the influential 'Italy: New Domestic Landscape' exhibition.
